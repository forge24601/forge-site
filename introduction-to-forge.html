What Is Your Legacy?

The FORGE Mentor Class builds mentors. Introduction to FORGE is for every man who lives in a FORGE dorm, and it is not a mentor class. It is about the man himself: knowing yourself, being a man of your word and what integrity means, having a purpose, and the legacy you leave in your dorm, your family and the men around you.

The Class

Twelve Sessions in Three Parts

Know Yourself (Sessions 1–4), A Man of Your Word (5–8), and Purpose and Legacy (9–12).

Session 1
Thinking Controls Behavior
Teach the idea the whole class is built on: thinking drives feelings and actions. Help each man see his own automatic thoughts, and give him the thinking report as a tool to slow them down.
Session 2
Thinking Errors
Name the eight thinking errors, the habits of thought that bend the facts and push a man toward harm, and help each man find the two or three he falls into most.
Session 3
The Anger Beneath the Anger
Help each man see that his anger usually covers something more painful, name the feelings his anger protects, and write privately about one time it happened, so that pain stops running him from the dark.
Session 4
Coping and Safety
Help each man tell safe coping from unsafe coping, build his own Safe Coping Card, and know when what he is carrying calls for more help than this class can give.
Session 5
Personal Accountability
Help each man understand what accountability is and isn't, see honestly where he stands on the accountability spectrum, and write a statement that owns one harm he caused, with no excuses and no "but."
Session 6
Integrity Under Pressure
Define integrity as values, words and actions that match, teach four tests a man can run on any hard choice, and practice them on dilemmas where doing right costs something.
Session 7
Making Amends and Moving Forward
Teach that keeping your word includes repairing it when you broke it. Help each man see the full ripple of the harm he caused, know an apology from real amends, and begin his impact awareness letter.
Session 8
Empathy Development
Teach that empathy is a skill a man can rebuild, in three kinds: understanding, feeling, and being moved to help. Help each man practice seeing one situation through four sets of eyes, and start to see how his own actions land on other people.
Session 9
Service Over Self
Teach Service Over Self, the principle FORGE is built on: what service is, what it is not, and why it sets a man free. Help each man find specific acts of service in his own dorm and commit to the kind of man he is becoming.
Session 10
Finding Purpose
Help each man find his why: the difference between goals and purpose, Viktor Frankl's lesson that no one can take away the freedom to choose what your suffering means, and a letter from the man he is becoming.
Session 11
The Stakeholder Identity
Move each man from "it's their system" to "what part is mine." Teach what a stakeholder is and what he actually controls, and have each man write five specific actions he will take in his own dorm, read aloud to the circle as his witnesses.
Session 12
What Is Your Legacy?
Bring the question that ends every session to the center: help each man see that he is leaving a legacy right now, and write his own legacy statement about what he chooses to build.
↻

Join Any Week

Men move into a dorm one at a time, so the class never waits for a group to fill. A new man has a short Orientation with a mentor, then joins at the next session and goes round until he has done all twelve. Every session stands on its own.

◯

Make-ups Are Live

A missed session is made up in person, on a designated make-up day, with other men in the room. Watching a video never counts: sharing experiences with other men is the point.

★

The Legacy Question

Every session ends with “What does today have to do with your legacy?” and one line in the man’s book. By his twelfth session those lines are his legacy statement. He reads it to the circle the day he finishes, and the group hands him his certificate.

About FORGE

FORGE is a peer-led, evidence-based mentorship program designed to build a safer, more stable dorm culture by training men to lead through service, accountability, and conflict reduction.

"To turn time served into time that serves others."
