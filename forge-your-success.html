The FORGE Your Success Elevator Speech

Every class opens with the men saying it together. It keeps the heart of the FORGE elevator speech, in the voice of a man who is home:

“FORGE — Facilitating Opportunities for Reentry, Growth and Empowerment. I’m home, and I’m building a new life — one where I lead by example, keep my word, and make a positive impact on my family and my community. My goal is simple: to turn time served into time that serves others. My purpose: to show what a second chance looks like — through honest work, real accountability and service — and to help the next man coming home do the same.”

The Cycle

Orientation and Twelve Classes

The Reentry Home takes residents as beds open, not as a group, so the class runs as a cycle. A new resident joins at whatever class comes next; every class stands on its own.

Orientation
Landing
Land the resident in the house: make the house agreement and the FORGE commitment, write the first Reentry Plan across all eight areas, and pin down the identification documents, the first supervision check-in and the first week.
Class 1
Thinking Under Pressure
Give every resident the core FORGE tool (the Think-Feel-Act cycle, the thinking report and STOP) and use it on the pressures of life after release.
Class 2
The Officer and Other Authority
Give every resident the FORGE position on authority (respect the role, even when you disagree with the person), the composure to hold it under pressure, and the habits that protect a plan with a supervision officer: exact conditions, early arrival, a written record, and the call before.
Class 3
Getting Hired: Your Story in Two Minutes
Help every resident tell a prospective employer about his record honestly and briefly (what happened, what changed, what he offers now), practice it in a mock interview, and know where to look for second-chance employers.
Class 4
Keeping the Job
Give every resident the FORGE tools for the hard moments of keeping a job: calling in properly, taking correction from a supervisor, working out conflict with a coworker, and deciding about a job on purpose instead of quitting in the moment.
Class 5
Money on the Outside
Give every resident the money basics FORGE teaches its mentors (budgeting, banking, debt traps and saving) and put them to work on a first paycheck out here.
Class 6
Coming Home to Family
Help residents meet the family they came home to, not the one they pictured; take their roles back slowly; and use the FORGE accountability conversation to own their part with someone they hurt, without demanding forgiveness.
Class 7
Old Friends, Old Places
Help residents see their relationships and the old places honestly, decide before the pressure hits, say no without starting a fight, and start building new circles.
Class 8
On Guard
Help residents understand why life outside can feel overwhelming after prison, notice where they are on the escalation curve and which survival response they go to, use breathing, grounding and STOP to come back to the present, and know when and where to get help.
Class 9
Recovery and the Stages of Change
Give every resident the Stages of Change and the support network map, and use them on the triggers of the first months home: what to expect, what to do when the pull comes, what to do after a slip, and who to call.
Class 10
When It Goes Wrong
Give every resident a way to handle a setback (own it, tell it early, take the next right step, ask for help) and teach the group how to respond when the setback is another resident's.
Class 11
Giving Back
Show why giving back is part of staying out, give every resident the four OARS skills, and practice using them with a new resident who wants to leave the house.
Class 12
Your Next Address
Help every resident plan the move out of the Reentry Home (a place to go, a backup, a savings target, what landlords look at, and how to stay connected) and keep the routines and people that work.

Every Class

  • Opening circleOne win and one challenge since last class.
  • Reentry Plan checkIn pairs: one step done, one step next.
  • TeachThe FORGE tool, explained from the beginning, then applied to life after release.
  • PracticeRole-play in the FORGE simulation format: set the scene, try it, freeze, coach, replay.
  • Commit and closeOne action for this week, written into the Reentry Plan and said out loud.
↻

Twice Through

A resident who stays about six months goes through the cycle twice: first as a participant, learning each tool and using it on his own Reentry Plan; then as a peer helper, sharing what he tried, playing the hard part in role-plays and coaching newer residents. Know yourself, build others, lead and serve: FORGE in miniature.

📖

A Book He Keeps

Every man gets his own copy of FORGE Your Success: A guide for men coming home: a chapter for every class, written for a man reading on his own, with the idea, the tool step by step, examples from life after release, the traps to watch for, pages to write in, and what to keep doing after he leaves the house. At the back: every tool on a few pages, the numbers he’ll need, and a reading list.

About FORGE

FORGE is a peer-led, evidence-based mentorship program designed to build a safer, more stable dorm culture by training men to lead through service, accountability, and conflict reduction.

"To turn time served into time that serves others."
