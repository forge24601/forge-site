Youth Roundtable

A FORGE dorm class for the younger men: eight Roundtables on value, voice, respect, the future, family and leadership

Facilitator Guide (PDF) Roundtable Journal (PDF)

A Dorm Class

A Seat at the Table

Many younger men came in young, carry long sentences, and hear more about what they did than about what they are worth. FORGE facilitators said what this class is for: offer them some kind of hope, and guide young men away from gang life and toward building as good a life as they can, even inside. Youth Roundtable does it by talking, planning and doing together.

The Class

Eight Roundtables

Built for younger men, open to any man in the dorm. A group starts together at Roundtable 1 and finishes together at Roundtable 8.

Roundtable 1
What Is Value?
Open the class, make the group's agreements, and help each man name the value he brings, so the next seven weeks have something to build on.
Roundtable 2
Finding Your Voice
Help each man see what makes it hard to speak up, practice standing up for himself with respect, and use his voice at the table by sharing one thing he wishes people understood about him.
Roundtable 3
Game Day: Leading a Team
Plan a Game Day for the dorm, with every man on a committee, so the group practices leading a team on a real project with real deadlines.
Roundtable 4
The Lunch Meeting: Friendship and Healthy Relationships
Check on Game Day, set the tournament calendar for the weeks ahead with leads for every event, and talk honestly about friendship: what a real friend does, how to know when you're being used, and what kind of friend each man wants to be.
Roundtable 5
Respect: Three Kinds
Help each man tell real respect from fear, name the three kinds of respect, and choose one place to show more of it this week.
Roundtable 6
Your Future: Goals and Aspirations
Help each man picture who he's becoming in five, three and one years, set one real goal for this year with a first step, and start a vision board, in a way that works whatever his sentence.
Roundtable 7
Family Ties and a Work Ethic
Help each man choose the people he'll stay connected to and one step to take with each, name one positive connection in the dorm, and pick one part of his work ethic to build.
Roundtable 8
Leadership Starts with You
Help each man see that leadership starts with him, without a title; choose one problem he can help solve; look back at what he built in eight weeks; and leave with a next step, and a certificate if he completed.
◯

Every Roundtable

  • 10 minOpening Circle. Opening words said together, then a check-in with the talking piece.
  • 10 minFrame the Topic. A story, a question, a short idea.
  • 25 minThe Roundtable. Open talk around the table. The facilitator talks least.
  • 10 minJournal. Each man works his page for the day.
  • 5 minClose. What he’s taking with him, and the week’s challenge.
♙

Game Day and Tournaments

In Roundtable 3 the group plans a Game Day for the whole dorm, with every man on a committee. In Roundtable 4 it sets a calendar of tournaments for the weeks after: spades, bowling, a spelling bee, a holiday tournament. The men lead them. Staff approves every event, and there is no betting, ever.

✓

Completing the Class

A man completes Youth Roundtable by attending at least 6 of the 8 Roundtables, including Roundtable 8, and taking a real part in Game Day or a tournament. He gets a certificate at Roundtable 8, and the facilitators give the list of men who completed to the staff sponsor for their files.

About FORGE

FORGE is a peer-led, evidence-based mentorship program designed to build a safer, more stable dorm culture by training men to lead through service, accountability, and conflict reduction.

"To turn time served into time that serves others."
